# Next experiment: one balanced-batch PCD probe at cursor 2

**Recommendation.** Test a single, deterministic DB- and system-balanced batch at the hash-bound cursor-2 state, retaining the existing chemistry-primary PCD direction and the already validated componentwise Armijo rule. This is the lowest-cost test that separates a sample-sensitive PCD ray from a persistent direction-selection failure without changing objective definitions, PCD, or its priority order.

## Why this fits the evidence

The previous finite-step issue is already falsified for one case: the original AE17/H2 step is rescued on the same PCD ray at `alpha0/32`, and two adaptive updates decrease all three same-sample losses. At cursor 2, however, `g_chem·(-d_PCD) > 0`, so no positive scalar shortening can help. The same raw gradients admit a strict common descent vector, with convex-hull weights `(chem, exc, op)=(0.001419, 0, 0.998581)`. Thus this batch is not Pareto-critical, but the PCD ray fails chemistry. That witness diagnoses the direction at this sample; it does not show whether the direction failure persists under better sampling.

Sampling is a plausible first lever: the checkpoint has only two accepted updates, cursor 2 is a single NCCE31/BH pair, and the matched 27-panel chemistry ratios have median `1.0145`, p90 `2.4604`, and only 11/27 wins, while E_xc and operator win 27/27. These observations motivate one balanced audit; they do not establish generalization or a stable variance estimate.

Liu and Vicente’s stochastic multi-gradient analysis shows that a nonlinear multiobjective direction can be biased even when each component gradient estimator is unbiased ([author paper](https://www.mat.uc.pt/~lnv/papers/smg-moo.pdf), published in *Annals of Operations Research* 339, 1119–1148). Chen et al. analyze how larger batches mitigate stochastic conflict-avoiding-direction bias, while also documenting a generalization trade-off ([JMLR 25, 2024](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume25/23-1287/23-1287.pdf), §§4 and Appendix B). These results motivate a batch-size falsification for this one snapshot; they are not guarantees for PCD’s EMA-normalized direction.

## Minimal protocol and gates

1. Restore the immutable cursor-2 checkpoint (`a53f4680…e3670c`) and its manifest (`550f2248…bf928`). Freeze parameters, buffers, RNG, sample identities, and the **pre-update** PCD EMA (`t=2`). First reproduce the recorded one-pair gradients, adverse chemistry slope, and zero-trial stop.
2. On that exact state, form one fixed balanced audit batch from the existing panel: all 27 reactions (three per each of nine databases) and each of the 15 distinct mRKS systems once. Preserve the existing per-item chemistry, E_xc, and AO-operator loss functions and average within each objective; do not change task weights, units, targets, variant/system identities, or PCD settings. Compute the three batch-mean gradients once and run canonical PCD once on a copy of the frozen EMA (`tau=.02`, `beta=.999`, `eps=1e-8`). Log raw norms, cosines, PCD joint dots, and the three raw-loss slopes for `Delta0=-6.632573669086685e-7*d`.
3. If any slope is nonnegative/nonfinite, run **no line search and no update**. Record whether the batch gradients still admit a strict common-descent certificate as a diagnosis only. If all slopes are strictly negative, evaluate the same frozen direction on the same fixed batch at `t=1, 1/2, …, 2^-20`, with `c=1e-4`, `rho=.5`, RNG/buffers restored per trial, and a float32 no-op guard. Accept only the largest representable trial that satisfies componentwise Armijo and strict actual decrease for chemistry, E_xc, and operator. Report every baseline/trial loss, slope, predicted and actual reduction, ratio, margin, norm, and item/database-level chemistry change.
4. This is a one-update shadow experiment, not a new training run. Commit the candidate and advance EMA once only in the shadow state if the gate passes; otherwise restore the exact checkpoint. Report the balanced-batch losses and the paired changes on the existing panel, with no broader success claim.

**Falsification signatures.** A balanced-batch PCD direction with three negative slopes and a finite all-three Armijo step supports sample-sensitive direction selection; a chemistry decrease on the 27-reaction mean is the required success, not merely secondary-task improvement. If chemistry remains non-descent while the larger-batch raw gradients still certify a strict common descent direction, batch enlargement did not cure the selected-ray failure. If slopes are all negative but no finite representable step passes, the failure is instead step/model behavior and warrants a trust-region follow-up.

## Why not the other mechanisms first

Fliege–Svaiter steepest descent provides a literature-native common-descent direction, but its method assumes no priority ordering or weights ([2000, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s001860000043)); the current witness is almost entirely operator-supported. Replacing PCD with that symmetric direction would not preserve chemistry priority. BBDMO changes direction construction to address objective imbalance and Armijo steps that become very small ([Chen, Tang & Yang, *EJOR* 311, 196–209, 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0377221723003028)); only one five-backtrack rescue is known, while the current stop has an adverse slope and no trials. Multiobjective trust-region methods use vector predicted/actual reduction and radius updates ([Carrizo, Lotito & Maciel, *Mathematical Programming* 159, 339–369, 2016](https://ri.conicet.gov.ar/bitstream/11336/58911/2/CONICET_Digital_Nro.9aad6520-291a-41f8-b2b8-540bc5ab6721_A.pdf)), but a radius cannot make the current PCD ray descend chemistry; model/subproblem machinery is premature before the balanced-batch direction is tested.

The PCD source paper describes its intended hierarchy explicitly ([Varam & AlHajri, TMLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29521)); this experiment keeps that method and its hyperparameters fixed. No family is assumed to win.
