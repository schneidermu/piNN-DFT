PCD’s Proposition 4.7 is explicitly K=2. With active constraint and unit normalized gradients it gives primary slope `1 + tau*c − c²`; this is not a K=3 threshold. For K=3, use the canonical QP and measured slopes. At tau zero, the QP is the Euclidean projection of the normalized chemistry gradient onto `{d: g̃_exc·d ≥ 0, g̃_op·d ≥ 0}`. Secondary slopes may be zero. For this cone projection, `g̃_chem·d = ||d||² ≥ 0`; the paper’s Appendix C.2 also says zero direction implies Pareto stationarity with positive primary weight, while Pareto stationarity alone need not imply zero direction. PCD’s claims concern its normalized direction and idealized first-order step, not general stochastic training or finite-step panel transfer. [PCD paper, §§4.1–4.7 and App. C.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29521v2).

Fliege–Svaiter’s native steepest direction is the negative minimum-norm point in the raw-gradient convex hull. Away from Pareto criticality, it has strict common descent and supports a componentwise Armijo search with dyadic steps; it assumes no objective ordering. Existence of a strict common-descent direction is invariant to positive objective rescaling, but the raw-gradient minimum-norm direction can change under such rescaling. Keep this symmetric construction as the fallback only if the PCD geometry gate fails. [Fliege & Svaiter (2000)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s001860000043).

Full-cycle stochastic-gradient estimation is the conditional next hypothesis only if the selected PCD direction passes same-batch Armijo but fails the paired-panel gate; Mercier et al. analyze expectation objectives with SMGDA, not this EMA-normalized PCD diagnostic. PMGDA needs an explicit preference constraint or region of interest; its correction phase allows bounded objective deterioration while reducing preference error. EPO targets a specified objective ratio and uses controlled ascent. Neither supplies the current chemistry-first, strict-all-three ray with no new preference choice.
