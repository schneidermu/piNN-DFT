## Why smaller-`tau` PCD is the first test

PCD projects the normalized primary gradient onto the intersection of secondary-progress half-spaces. Its `tau` controls the required secondary margin: lowering positive `tau` expands the feasible set toward the primary gradient while retaining strictly positive idealized first-order progress on each nonstationary secondary. This directly tests whether the current chemistry slope is caused by excessive secondary pressure, while retaining the existing hierarchy.

The primary paper's Proposition 4.7 is **only for `K=2`**. In its active, unit-normalized case, primary progress is `1 + tau*c − c²`; for conflict `c<0`, it stays positive only when `tau < (1−c²)/(−c)`. This is a two-objective threshold, not a reusable universal `tau0` for this chemistry/E_xc/operator `K=3` state. The paper gives a KKT solve for `K=3`, but no analogous one-scalar primary-descent threshold. Therefore compute the actual three slopes for every tested `tau`; do not infer them from pairwise cosines. Its guarantees concern the normalized constructed direction and idealized step, not an arbitrary adaptive-optimizer displacement. Check slopes on the actual proposed displacement. [Varam & AlHajri, *Priority-Constrained Descent* (TMLR 2026), paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29521).

## Alternatives

- **B, Fliege–Svaiter/MGDA:** a valid symmetric fallback. The stored strict common-descent witness proves this batch admits one, and the native minimum-norm convex-hull direction is the canonical common-descent construction. Its weights encode no chemistry priority; the witness itself puts about `99.86%` on operator. Use it if no tested positive `tau` yields three negative slopes. For a strict descent direction and differentiable fixed-batch losses, the paper's componentwise Armijo backtracking from `t=1` with geometric halving has a sufficiently small accepted step. Its convergence results do not transfer to PCD or stochastic training. [Fliege & Svaiter (2000)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s001860000043).
- **C, full-cycle gradient estimate then PCD:** useful only if a same-batch PCD direction passes Armijo but fails the paired panel, or evidence later shows sample-composition instability. No current result isolates variance, and a full 27-entry accumulation costs more while changing the gradient estimate. [Mercier, Poirion & Désidéri (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejor.2018.05.064).
- **D, PMGDA/EPO or trust region:** not first. PMGDA needs an explicit preference constraint and predict/correct steps; EPO deliberately allows controlled ascent while steering to a preferred Pareto point. Neither is the smallest way to keep all three directional derivatives negative while honoring the existing chemistry hierarchy. A multiobjective trust region addresses model-versus-actual decrease, whereas cursor 2 already fails at the first-order direction test. [PMGDA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09492); [EPO](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/mahapatra20a.html); [Carrizo, Lotito & Maciel (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-015-0962-6).

## Frozen-state protocol and gates

1. Restore the exact cursor-2 checkpoint and batch; verify the checkpoint hash from the result JSON and reproduce the three baseline slopes. Keep parameters, batch, RNG, EMA, optimizer, and scheduler fixed during all trials.
2. Reuse the three raw gradients and the cursor's existing EMA normalization. Recompute the **canonical `K=3` PCD QP** at `tau = 0.01, 0.005, 0.0025, ...`, in that order; retain `tau=0.02` as the reproduction control. Stop after the first trial with three strictly negative raw slopes and a passing finite step, or when the QP margin falls below its numerical tolerance. Report every tested `tau`, active set, actual displacement slopes, and selected learning fraction. Do not replace the QP with a custom gradient blend.
3. For a candidate with all three slopes negative, run the existing shared, componentwise Armijo search from its native initial proposal, halving the scalar multiplier. Require strict actual decrease and Armijo for chemistry, E_xc, and operator on the same frozen batch. Accept the largest passing fraction; commit nothing during diagnosis.
4. Only after a same-batch pass, evaluate that one candidate on the fixed cursor-2 panel. Success requires strict improvement in all three panel objectives; report chemistry first. A panel failure means the direction rescue did not transfer. Only then consider the pre-existing conditional C fallback. A no-slope candidate is rejected before line search, and Armijo cannot repair it.

This is a local falsification only. It establishes neither population improvement nor training success. The previous T2 response curve already showed that vector Armijo can rescue a finite-step overshoot (`j=5`, `t=1/32`); cursor 2 presents a different failure, a non-common PCD direction, so the next test must change the direction construction through PCD's existing `tau` knob.
