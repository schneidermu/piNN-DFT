## Why lower positive τ is the first probe

PCD projects the EMA-normalized primary gradient onto constraints requiring secondary first-order progress. Its paper defines `τ∈[0,1]` as the normalized secondary margin; reducing positive τ relaxes secondary pressure and moves the projection closer to the primary gradient. That directly tests whether `τ=.02` causes the cursor-2 chemistry slope while retaining chemistry as the anchor and positive first-order guarantees for smooth secondaries. The paper’s Proposition 4.7 is explicitly **K=2 only**: under its active, unit-normalized two-gradient assumptions, primary slope is `1 + τc − c²`. It is not a K=3 guarantee for chemistry plus two secondaries. The paper does give the K=3 active-set/closed-form QP and includes a K=3 application, so this frozen grid is valid to test, but only the measured raw slopes establish chemistry descent here. At `τ=0`, PCD’s constraints become secondary non-ascent and its fixed-point result becomes MGDA-style Pareto stationarity; that does not ensure strict E_xc/operator descent, so keep the candidates positive. PCD’s claims concern the normalized direction and idealized first-order step, not finite actual-loss decrease or general stochastic-training convergence. [Varam & AlHajri, TMLR 2026, paper §§4.5–4.7, App. C.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29521).

## Alternatives

| Option | Assessment for this cursor |
|---|---|
| **A. Smaller-τ PCD + existing Armijo** | **Run first.** Preserves the primary/secondary roles and changes one scalar in the current direction rule. The grid is falsifiable: report every slope and Armijo result; no positive-τ point passes all three slopes means A failed at this state. Choose the largest passing τ to retain as much secondary margin as possible. |
| **B. Fliege–Svaiter/MGDA + Armijo** | Valid diagnostic fallback if A fails. The stored raw-gradient witness `(chem, exc, op)=(0.001419116, 0, 0.998580884)` gives strict dots `(-0.09194, -879.07, -0.09194)`, so the exact batch has a common descent direction. But this symmetric construction has no chemistry preference, and the witness is operator-dominated with a comparatively weak chemistry slope. It tests common-descent feasibility, not priority-preserving optimization. The native direction is the negative minimum-norm point of the raw-gradient convex hull; start its line search at native `t=1`, halve, and do not reuse PCD’s `alpha0`. Fliege–Svaiter explicitly assumes no objective ordering or weights. [Fliege & Svaiter (2000)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s001860000043). **Access note:** the publisher page and abstract were available, but its full 2000 article text was paywalled in this review; exact section-level Armijo constants could not be independently checked there. |
| **C. Full-cycle/balanced gradients + canonical PCD** | Reserve for a batch-pass/panel-fail result, or evidence that lower τ only helps the single reaction row. This preserves PCD’s hierarchy but changes the estimator and costs one full immutable 27-entry training-manifest cycle. Two accepted updates and one chemistry panel screen do not establish variance as the cause. Use existing reductions and weights; keep panel rows evaluation-only. Stochastic multi-gradient methods study expectation-valued objectives, but do not guarantee this PCD estimator’s direction. [Mercier, Poirion & Désidéri (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejor.2018.05.064). |
| **D. PMGDA/EPO or trust region** | Defer. PMGDA’s prediction step descends all objectives while tangent to a chosen preference constraint, but its correction step reduces the preference error while allowing bounded objective deterioration; it requires a new preference function and extra optimization phase. EPO likewise uses controlled ascent to reach a preference-specific Pareto point. Neither is a minimal drop-in strict all-three descent test. [Zhang, Lin & Zhang (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09492); [Mahapatra & Rajan (2020)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/mahapatra20a.html). A trust region addresses unreliable finite-step model predictions, while the cursor-2 PCD direction already has an adverse first-order chemistry slope; it cannot make shortening that same ray produce chemistry descent. The earlier line search already rescued the separate overshoot case. |

## Gates and interpretation

1. Verify frozen checkpoint, source, batch, and EMA identities; reproduce the recorded `τ=.02` slopes before screening alternatives.
2. On identical raw gradients and a copy of the same pre-update EMA, compute canonical PCD at each positive τ. Record active constraints, all three raw-loss directional slopes, and QP validity. Require three robustly negative slopes; otherwise run no trial for that τ.
3. Search each eligible candidate with the existing PCD vector Armijo rule on the unchanged exact batch. Accept only a representable step satisfying strict actual decrease and componentwise Armijo for all three objectives. If none passes within the guard, reject without state mutation.
4. On the first passing candidate in descending τ order, evaluate the paired cursor-2 panel. Call this probe successful only if each of chemistry, E_xc, and operator strictly improves on the base panel; chemistry is the primary reported result. A batch pass without panel improvement triggers C as the next sampling diagnostic, not a claim of training success.

A positive-τ candidate with three negative slopes and an Armijo pass supports secondary pressure at `.02` as the local direction cause. If no positive candidate passes the chemistry slope, run the symmetric B diagnostic only as a bounded feasibility check; label its lack of hierarchy explicitly. No result here supports a long-run gate, general PCD failure, or full-90 operator readiness.
