## Evidence and literature fit

At cursor 2 (NCCE31 reaction 0 / BH / `level2_mura`), canonical PCD at \(\tau=0.02\) has the recorded chemistry proposal slope `+0.0093064049`, while E_xc and operator are `−0.4226231444` and `−0.0000329078`; it is not a common-descent ray. Yet the raw-gradient simplex witness gives strict common descent, with weights `(0.001419116, 0, 0.998580884)` and dots `(−0.0919399, −879.071583, −0.0919399)`. This proves directional feasibility on the batch, not a finite Armijo step or panel benefit. The paired panel chemistry median ratio is `1.01452039` (11/27 rows improve); E_xc and operator ratios are `0.64866504` and `0.95511256` (27/27 each). These are rows over 15 cached systems, with only two accepted training updates.

PCD projects the chemistry gradient onto secondary-progress half-spaces, choosing the feasible direction closest to that primary gradient. Positive \(\tau\) imposes positive first-order margins on nonzero smooth secondaries; reducing it weakens their pressure and favors chemistry. At \(\tau=0\), those margins become non-strict, so it cannot alone meet the three-strict-slope gate. The PCD paper’s Proposition 4.7 gives the primary-slope formula `1 + τc − c²` only for **K=2**, unit-normalized active gradients; it must not be asserted as a K=3 guarantee. The paper gives K=3 QP handling, but its guarantees are local to the normalized direction and idealized first-order step; it gives no convergence theorem for the deployed optimizer. Thus lower-\(\tau\) recovery is a hypothesis to test, not a promised result. [Varam & AlHajri, PCD (2026), §§4.1–4.7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29521).
