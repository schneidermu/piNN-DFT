## Why this is the smallest useful test

At cursor 2, the canonical `tau=0.02` PCD proposal has slopes `(chem, exc, op) = (+0.0093064049, −0.4226231444, −0.0000329078)`. Its chemistry slope is adverse, so shortening that ray cannot help. The same gradients are not Pareto-stationary: a strict common-descent witness has convex-hull weights `(0.001419116, 0, 0.998580884)` and update-direction dots `(-0.0919399, -879.071583, -0.0919399)`. That witness proves a common direction exists, but puts almost all convex weight on the operator gradient and gives no chemistry priority.

PCD keeps chemistry as the structural anchor and projects its normalized chemistry gradient onto the secondary-progress halfspaces. Lowering positive `tau` weakens only the required secondary margin; it keeps task order, EMA normalization, loss definitions, and the PCD QP unchanged. For a feasible PCD QP, `tau>0` gives a positive first-order margin for each nonzero secondary, while the chemistry sign must be checked at this K=3 state. The strict witness establishes feasibility for positive margins at this cursor.

The PCD paper's Proposition 4.7 is **K=2 only**. For an active constraint and unit normalized gradients with cosine `c<0`, it gives primary descent iff `tau < tau0 = (1−c²)/(−c)`. That threshold is not a K=3 result and cannot be applied to this two-secondary state; the paper gives a K=3 closed form for the active-set multipliers, not a general primary-preservation threshold. Its Sec. 4.7 also limits guarantees to the normalized constructed direction and idealized first-order step; it proves no convergence theorem for the deployed optimizer loop. [Varam & AlHajri, PCD, arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29521).

There is a local reason to expect a sufficiently small positive `tau` to work here. In PCD's sign convention, negate the recorded strict descent witness to obtain a vector with positive dots against all three gradients. It is feasible for the `tau=0` secondary halfspaces and has a positive chemistry dot. The projection of the chemistry gradient onto that cone is therefore nonzero and has positive chemistry dot; by continuity, the PCD projection retains that sign for sufficiently small positive margins. Then the positive `tau` constraints give strict secondary dots. This is a geometric inference from the recorded witness and PCD's projection formulation, not Proposition 4.7 or a numerical guarantee that one of the six tested values will pass.

## Frozen-state protocol and gates

1. Restore the seed-41 cursor-2 checkpoint, exact NCCE31 reaction 0 / BH / `level2_mura` batch, pre-update EMA at `t=2`, model buffers, and RNG. Recompute the three existing raw gradients once. First replay `tau=0.02`; require the recorded slopes above at the receipt's recorded precision. A mismatch is an identity/replay failure.
2. Reuse those gradients and one scratch copy of the PCD EMA update for every tau. Evaluate the canonical PCD QP at all six values, `{0, 0.001, 0.0025, 0.005, 0.01, 0.02}`, and record each raw-gradient dot with the proposed PCD parameter direction. Do not assume the chemistry slope is monotone in tau for K=3.
3. For each positive candidate with three finite, strictly negative slopes, in ascending-tau order, evaluate the unchanged three raw losses on the same frozen batch using `Delta_tau = −alpha0*d_tau`, `alpha0 = 6.632573669086685e-7`. Apply the validated shared-fraction sequence `t=1, 1/2, …, 2^-20`; accept only a representable trial that has strict actual decrease and componentwise Armijo for all three losses (`c=1e-4`, `rho=0.5`). Restore the exact snapshot before each trial. Stop after the first accepted positive tau; larger-tau line searches are then unnecessary for the predeclared selection rule. The 20-halving/no-op limits are implementation guards, not paper defaults. The Armijo values are supported by [Mita, Fukuda & Yamashita (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10898-019-00802-0); the Fliege–Svaiter method itself uses a componentwise Armijo search with a dyadic sequence, without prescribing these numeric values ([Fliege & Svaiter (2000)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s001860000043)).
4. Select the smallest positive tau in the fixed list that passes both the strict-slope gate and same-batch Armijo gate. This selects the least tested secondary margin, hence the most chemistry-anchored passing PCD candidate among this fixed list. Tau zero is diagnostic only: its secondary constraints allow zero slope and do not provide PCD's positive secondary-margin guarantee.

If no positive tau passes, stop without training. Report all six slope triplets, Armijo trials through the selected value (or exhaustion), and whether float32 movement or the finite search guard limited a candidate. A pass establishes one frozen-batch direction and finite step only; it makes no panel, population, or training-success claim.

## Candidate comparison

| Option | Decision |
|---|---|
| **A. Smaller fixed PCD tau + vector Armijo** | **Run first.** It changes one canonical PCD scalar, preserves chemistry as the anchor, and costs only small QP solves after the three frozen gradients are computed. |
| **B. Fliege–Svaiter / MGDA direction + Armijo** | The raw-gradient witness already proves this can give strict first-order decrease, but its weights are `(chem .001419, exc 0, op .998581)`. Fliege–Svaiter assumes no objective order or preference, so it is the wrong first choice when preserving chemistry priority matters. Use only as a later nonpriority reference. |
| **C. Full-cycle/balanced gradients + canonical PCD** | This tests sample-composition sensitivity and costs another full gradient accumulation. The current one-sample gradients already admit a common-descent direction, so more sampling is not needed to establish local feasibility. Stochastic MGDA literature motivates full-batch checks but does not prove a benefit for EMA-normalized PCD ([Liu & Vicente, 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.04472)). Defer until a one-step PCD rescue fails a separately defined transfer question. |
| **D. PMGDA/EPO or trust region** | PMGDA adds a preference constraint and predictor-corrector search toward a preferred Pareto solution ([Zhang et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09492)); EPO needs a preference vector and can allow controlled ascent outside its pure-descent mode ([Mahapatra & Rajan, ICML 2020](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/mahapatra20a.html)). Neither preference target is specified here, and both are broader replacements than the PCD tau test. A multiobjective trust region has vector predicted/actual reduction and radius updates ([Carrizo, Lotito & Maciel, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-015-0962-6)); reserve it for a direction with negative slopes whose finite Armijo steps repeatedly fail. It cannot repair the present positive chemistry slope by shrinking the same ray. |
